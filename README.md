# SceneRelay

SceneRelay is a clean-room concept for a focused platform where people can share
short scenes, skits, and creative prompts, receive useful feedback, and find
collaborators. It intentionally preserves only the broad product category—not
another product's name, copy, branding, assets, screens, or implementation.

## Reboot direction

| Area | Original approach to take |
| --- | --- |
| Product name | **SceneRelay** — a distinct, descriptive working name. Run a trademark and domain review before launch. |
| Core promise | Turn an unfinished scene into a clearer next step: feedback, a collaborator, or a production plan. |
| Primary audience | Emerging sketch creators and small creator teams who want specific, fast feedback before publishing. |
| Tone | Constructive, creator-first, and practical—not a generic social feed. |
| Visual identity | Develop an original logo, typography, palette, illustrations, and UI component system from scratch. |

## Ideal customer profile

**Independent sketch creator or small comedy/video team**

- Produces short-form scenes or skits regularly, usually with limited time,
  budget, and collaborators.
- Needs timely, actionable feedback before recording or posting.
- Wants help finding performers, editors, writers, or locations without
  managing a large production workflow.
- Values a small, relevant creative community over broad engagement metrics.

### Number-one pain point

They cannot reliably turn an early idea into a publishable skit. Feedback is
often late, vague, or comes from people who are not the intended audience;
coordination then happens across disconnected chats and documents.

## Market gap to address

Most existing options solve only one part of the workflow:

- Social platforms distribute finished videos but do not provide structured
  pre-production critique.
- Generic collaboration tools organize work but do not understand scene beats,
  comedic timing, casting, or audience fit.
- Freelance marketplaces help hire people but make it difficult to assemble a
  lightweight, recurring creative circle.

SceneRelay should close this gap with a creator-specific path from **draft →
feedback → collaborators → publish-ready plan**.

## Value-building features

1. **Structured scene review** — Let creators upload a script, beat sheet, or
   rough cut and request feedback by category: hook, pacing, clarity, punchline,
   casting, and production feasibility. Reviewers should leave anchored notes,
   not only likes or comments.
2. **Feedback matching** — Match each request with a small group of reviewers
   based on genre, intended audience, skill, and stated availability. Include a
   feedback deadline and a simple usefulness rating.
3. **Creative-circle workspace** — Keep the approved script, version history,
   assigned roles, shot list, and decision log in one shareable project space.
4. **Contributor matching** — Offer opt-in profiles for writers, actors,
   editors, and videographers, with capability tags, location/remote status,
   availability, and portfolio links. Make introductions consent-based.
5. **Publish-readiness checklist** — Convert a selected scene into an original
   production checklist: rights/permissions, cast confirmation, props, filming
   plan, captions, and launch assets. This makes the product valuable after the
   feedback phase.
6. **Learning loop** — After publishing, let creators record outcomes such as
   completion, retention, saves, and qualitative audience reaction. Use these
   private results to improve future review prompts—not to create a public
   vanity-metric feed.

## Clean-room and intellectual-property guardrails

An idea or broad product category can be a starting point, but copying a
specific product's protected expression can create risk. Build SceneRelay as an
independent product by following these rules:

- Do not reuse a prior product's name, logo, trademark, screenshots, artwork,
  UI layouts, written copy, source code, data, or distinctive feature wording.
- Write fresh requirements from the audience problem and workflow above; do not
  recreate screens from memory or reference material.
- Create new brand assets and original onboarding, taxonomy, interaction
  patterns, and marketing language.
- Document the sources for any assets, fonts, media, and open-source packages;
  honor their licenses.
- Before using the name publicly, obtain professional trademark and legal
  advice for the markets where the product will operate.

This brief is a product-direction document, not legal advice.
